Reilly Plaza (Food Lion) — 7.85 Acres Sold Subject to the Ground Lease — Broker Price Opinion

This report values the fee simple land interest in Reilly Plaza, three parcels totaling 7.85 acres owned by the MacIlwinen family, if sold subject to the 1977 ground lease to Reilly Associates, Ltd. (Faison). Faison subleases the 32,250 SF store to Food Lion. A buyer would collect the ground rent until the lease ends on March 3, 2037, and then take back the land and its redevelopment potential. The report also gives the value of the land as if vacant, as requested.

The $1,100,000 opinion is for the land owner’s position, sold subject to the ground lease, with the Food Lion building and the restaurant building left in place under the lease. Most of the value comes from getting the land back in about 10 years, not from the ground rent. The ground rent itself was not provided and is assumed at about $20,000 a year, from the 1995 estimate in the lease summary. The as-if-vacant value of $2,150,000 assumes no ground lease and no buildings.

Key lease dates

  • March 3, 2027. The second ground lease option ends. Faison must have exercised, or must exercise, the final 10-year option. With Food Lion committed to 2030, Faison is expected to renew. This should be confirmed with Faison now.
  • March 15, 2030. Food Lion’s current option ends. It renews automatically to 2035 unless Food Lion gives notice.
  • March 15, 2035. Food Lion’s last option ends. Faison would have about two years left to re-lease, with little reason to invest.
  • March 3, 2037. The ground lease ends at the outside date. The land comes back to the fee owner. This report assumes the buildings will be about 60 years old at that point and will be torn down.

Methodology

  • Land as if vacant. Sales comparison on price per square foot (shown per acre in the matrix), using closed sales of commercial land in west Fayetteville from 2023 to 2026, taken from county deed stamps. No comps came from the owner, Faison or the leasing materials.
  • Leased fee. The value a buyer would pay for the owner’s position: the present value of the ground rent to 3/3/2037 at 7%, plus the present value of the land coming back in 2037. The land is grown at 2.5% a year, less about $300,000 to tear down the buildings, and discounted at 9.5% for the 10-year wait and the unknowns in the lease.
  • Adjustments. Each land sale is adjusted for time (about 2% a year), size, location and site condition. The percentages and discount rates are the broker’s professional judgment.

The ground rent matters less than it seems: each $10,000 a year adds only about $70,000 of value. The bigger drivers are the discount rate and the land value in 2037. The low end of the range fits a buyer who sees more risk in the lease terms or the 2037 timing. The high end fits a higher actual ground rent, buildings that come back with some re-lease value, or a sale to Faison, which could merge the land and the leasehold.

Development potential

  • The 4.04-acre rear tract is the main upside. It is wooded, zoned CC, has about 445 ft on N Reilly Rd by GIS, and is marketed by Aston as about 4.41 acres available. It is part of the ground lease, so Faison, not the fee owner, controls it until 2037.
  • Any new building helps the fee owner now. If Faison builds or ground-leases the rear tract or the “future shops” space, the percentage rent in the ground lease may rise. How much depends on the paragraph 4 formula, which was not provided.
  • Faison or Aston is the most likely buyer. Owning both the land and the leasehold would let them re-lease or redevelop past 2037 and lend against the whole center. That buyer can pay toward the high end of the range.
  • Food Lion’s rights limit the parking field. The 189-space parking layout cannot change without Food Lion’s consent while its lease runs, which limits new pads in front of the store before 2035.
  • After 2037. A buyer would own a 7.85-acre signalized corner near the Reilly Rd gate to Fort Bragg. It could be re-tenanted with a grocer, split into pads along both roads, or redeveloped with the rear land as apartments or shops, subject to city approvals.

Counter-evidence & upside

  • The county value is much higher. The county assessed the three parcels at $3,290,368, including $1,871,943 for the buildings. That is a fee simple figure. It ignores the ground lease, and it counts buildings the fee owner does not control until 2037.
  • The buildings might be worth something in 2037. If the buildings come back to the fee owner and a grocer leases the store directly, even a fraction of Food Lion’s $185,000 rent would be worth more than the land. That is not assumed, because the reversion terms were not provided and the store will be 60 years old.
  • The rent could be higher than assumed. The $19,132 figure is a 1995 estimate. If the percentage rent now picks up BTJ Wings or other income, the rent could be $30,000–$40,000. The sensitivity table shows the effect.
  • Faison might not renew in 2027. If the final option is not exercised, the land would come back in March 2027, but the Food Lion sublease would also be at risk. That outcome could raise or lower the value and needs the full lease to judge.
  • Lower land sales. A 5.21-ac tract on S Reilly Rd sold for $300,000 (about $57,600 per acre) in 1/2025, and 49 acres on Raeford Rd sold for $4,300,000 (about $87,800 per acre) in 8/2026. Both were larger or less visible sites, but they show that interior commercial land trades well below corner pricing.
  • Higher land sale. The City paid about $491,000 per acre for 3.46 acres on Bonanza Dr in 5/2025 (C6). Public buyers often pay more, so it gets only 10% weight.

Limitations

  1. Incomplete lease. Only lease memoranda, amendments and a 1995 summary were reviewed. The current ground rent, the percentage-rent formula, renewal status and the reversion terms are assumed and must be confirmed.
  2. No inspection or survey. The property was not inspected. Acreage and frontage come from county GIS.
  3. Environmental. No environmental review was done. Grocery and restaurant sites can have grease, refrigerant or fuel issues that affect redevelopment.
  4. Comp data. Comp sizes and prices come from Cumberland County records and deed stamps and were not confirmed with buyers or sellers.
  5. Title and legal. No title search was done. Lease interpretation should be confirmed by the owner’s attorney.
